Understanding Benford's Law

Benford's Law states that in many real-world datasets, the first digit is more likely to be 1 than any other digit. In fact, about 30% of all numbers start with 1, while only 4.6% start with 9.

Why? Natural datasets grow exponentially (populations, prices, data sizes). When quantities grow exponentially, lower leading digits appear more frequently mathematically.

Fraud detection: When humans fabricate data, they tend to spread digits evenly (thinking each digit is equally likely). This creates a "signature" that deviates from Benford's Law, revealing the fraud.

Real examples: Census data, election vote totals, earthquake magnitudes, stock prices, and scientific measurements all follow Benford's Law closely. Fabricated expense reports, manipulated accounting data, and synthetic numbers do not.
